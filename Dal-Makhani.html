Dal Makhani (North Indian Classic)

Creamy slow-simmered whole urad dal blended with rajma, butter and cream — true comfort food when simmered slowly.

⭐ Rating4.5 ★
DifficultyMedium — slow-cook
Serves4
TimePrep 15 min (+soak) • Cook 45–60 min

Ingredients

Lentils & Beans

  • 1 cup whole black urad dal (sabut urad), soaked overnight
  • ¼ cup rajma (kidney beans), soaked overnight
  • 3–4 cups water (for cooking)
  • Salt to taste

For the Masala

  • 3 tbsp butter + 2 tbsp oil
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp ginger–garlic paste
  • 3 medium tomatoes, pureed
  • 1 tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 3–4 tbsp fresh cream (add more for richness)
  • A pinch kasuri methi (optional)
Tossing!

Method — Slow-cook style

1. Cook the dal and rajma

Drain & add to cooker: Drain the soaked urad dal and rajma. Add them to a pressure cooker with 3–4 cups of water and 1 tsp salt.

Pressure-cook: Cook for 6–8 whistles (times vary by cooker and bean softness). Aim until they are very soft — this is the base of the silky texture. After pressure releases, lightly mash a portion with the back of a ladle to help thicken the body.

2. Prepare the makhani base

Heat fat: Warm 3 tbsp butter + 2 tbsp oil in a heavy pan.

Sauté onions: Add chopped onions and cook on medium-low until deep golden brown — this slow browning brings natural sweetness and depth.

Add aromatics: Stir in ginger–garlic paste and cook until raw aroma disappears.

3. Add tomatoes & spices

Tomato stage: Pour in the pureed tomatoes. Add turmeric, red chilli, cumin and coriander powders. Cook until oil separates from the masala (important signal that masala is done).

4. Combine & simmer

Mix: Add the cooked dal-rajma mixture into the pan. Adjust consistency with extra water to reach creamy, flowing texture.

Slow simmer: Let the pot simmer at the lowest flame for 30–45 minutes — the long gentle simmer develops the classic richness. Stir occasionally and add butter/cream in intervals to layer the flavor.

5. Final touches

Finish: Sprinkle garam masala, a pinch of crushed kasuri methi, and a final knob of butter. Stir gently.

Serve: Serve piping hot with naan, jeera rice or rotis. For a smoky dhaba-style finish, use the coal-smoke technique (place hot coal in a small bowl with ghee inside the dal pot and cover for a few minutes).

Pro Tips

Slow simmering is the secret — the longer you cook gently, the creamier and more complex the flavors become. Use butter and cream liberally if you like a restaurant-style richness.

Notes & Serving Suggestions

Serve piping hot with buttered naan, jeera rice or rotis. A final drizzle of cream and a knob of butter at the end makes it feel luxurious. Refrigerate leftovers (up to 3 days) and reheat gently, adding a splash of water if thickened.

For smoky dhaba-style flavour: heat a small coal, place it in a tiny bowl with 1 tsp ghee, set inside the dal pot and cover immediately for 3–5 minutes to infuse smoke.
